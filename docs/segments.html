Study 03 / Independent analysis

Finding customer patterns that survive a second look.

An exploratory segmentation study with resampling, outlier sensitivity, and a deliberate limit on what customer clusters can claim.

01 / The question

Are these groups more than a tidy chart?

Customer clusters are easy to produce and easy to over-interpret. This study looks for broad spending patterns in a wholesale distributor’s customer base, then tests how sensitive those patterns are to the sample and its largest spenders.

Six annual spending categories define the feature space. Channel and region are kept out of clustering, so the customer labels cannot simply reproduce those existing classifications.

02 / Study design

Compress skew. Check stability.

Spending values are transformed with log1p and standardized before k-means. Five candidates, k = 2 through 6, are compared on silhouette score. Each fit uses 30 initializations and a fixed seed; k = 2 has the highest score.

For each of 100 bootstrap samples, the entire transformation and clustering pipeline is refit, then used to assign the original 440 customers. Adjusted Rand index measures agreement with the original partition without relying on arbitrary cluster labels.

How to read the stability result

Median ARI is 0.937; the central 95% of bootstrap results spans 0.675–1.000. These are resampling stability summaries, not estimates of future-customer prediction accuracy. Silhouette selection and evaluation both use this exploratory sample.

03 / The evidence

Two broad purchasing profiles emerge.

The grocery-led group contains 188 customers, with higher median grocery, milk, and detergents/paper spending. The other 252 customers spend more on fresh and frozen products. These names describe relative spending patterns; they are not verified customer personas.

Removing the highest 1% of customers by total spending and refitting gives an ARI of 0.973 on the retained customers. Channel, used only after fitting, provides another comparison: retail customers make up 71.3% of the grocery-led group and 3.2% of the other group.

04 / Interpretation

Stable enough to investigate, not to overstate.

A silhouette score of 0.29 indicates overlapping groups, even though assignments are relatively stable under resampling. Stable clusters are not necessarily sharp, causal, or commercially useful segments.

This is a small, cross-sectional dataset without repeat transactions, profit, or future outcomes. The study cannot establish customer lifetime value, churn, or the effect of a tailored offer.

The next step would be to validate these patterns on a later customer cohort, compare alternatives such as mixture models, and test whether segment-specific assortment or service changes improve a predeclared operational measure.

05 / Open the work

Reproduce the analysis.

Python 3.12, pinned analysis libraries, and a fixed random seed of 42. The first run downloads the source archive from UCI. The pipeline writes the metrics, predictions, and charts used on this page.

python -m venv .venv
# Activate .venv, then:
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python run.py segments
python -m unittest discover -s tests
